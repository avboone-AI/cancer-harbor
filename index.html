First 48-hour checklist

  1. Confirm the exact diagnosis, stage, biomarkers, and prior treatments.
  2. Ask whether molecular/genomic testing is complete and current.
  3. Collect recent scans, pathology, labs, treatment timeline, and notes.
  4. Search trials by cancer type, mutation, prior treatment, and location.

Start here

You have cancer. Now what?

The goal is not to become your own oncologist. The goal is to become organized enough to ask better questions, move faster, and make sure the full landscape of options is visible.

1

Name the disease precisely

Cancer type, subtype, stage, grade, biomarkers, mutations, and previous therapies all shape which treatments and trials may be relevant.

2

Build the source packet

Gather pathology reports, scan reports, lab trends, treatment dates, medication list, performance status notes, and genomic testing results.

3

Map standard options

Ask what the usual next treatment is, what it is expected to do, what alternatives exist, and what information would change the plan.

4

Search outside the building

Hospitals often know their own trials best. A separate search can reveal nearby, regional, national, virtual, or biomarker-specific opportunities.

Decision flow

When first-line treatment stops working

Progression confirmed Review scans, symptoms, and whether a biopsy is needed.
Re-check biology Ask about repeat molecular testing, resistance mutations, and new biomarkers.
Compare paths Standard treatment, off-label rationale, second opinion, clinical trial.
Trial outreach Contact coordinators with a concise history and ask about screening criteria.

Self-advocacy

Being an advocate means making the system easier to navigate.

Advocacy is not being difficult. It is keeping a clear record, asking what else should be considered, pushing for timely answers, and making sure each specialist sees the same facts.

Questions to ask

  • What evidence says this is the best next option?
  • Are there trials elsewhere that fit this cancer profile?
  • What biomarkers or test results would open or close options?
  • How quickly do we need to decide?

Records to control

  • Diagnosis and pathology reports
  • Genomic or biomarker test results
  • Prior therapies, dates, response, and side effects
  • Recent imaging and lab reports
